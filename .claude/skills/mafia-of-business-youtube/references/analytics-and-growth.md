# Choosing topics and titles

The operator posts each video by hand, so the pipeline has no view counts, retention curves or subscriber data, and the agent must never invent any. What follows is what the operator's own YouTube Studio showed on 2026-09-30, the only real numbers on record.

## What the channel's data said (2026-09-30)

- 19 videos, 11 subscribers, 412 views in 28 days, down ~84% on the period before.
- Best videos: the elevator brake (358 views), the transistor (269). Both were "the real story behind a thing", with a clear search term in the title.
- Weakest retention: videos averaging 0:22-0:26 of 6-8 minutes (clock, ATM). Stronger: stories holding 40-50% (Tesla, Mary Celeste).
- A month with no uploads (Aug 29 to Sep 26) and a change of topic after it.
- No Shorts, no playlists, almost no comments.

Read this as: distribution, not quality, is the bottleneck, and retention is what earns more distribution.

## What the agent can do

- Choose topics per `topic-strategy.md`; prefer a real story with a person, a stake and a named open question, and a search term people actually type.
- Write titles and thumbnail text from `thumbnail-and-metadata.md`; change one variable at a time when trying something new.
- Make the first 30 seconds earn the next five minutes (see `script-formula.md`): retention is the lever.
- Log every deliberate change in `reports/changelog.md` and any experiment in `reports/experiments.md`.
- Use operator notes on how a video performed when picking the next topic and titles.
- Resist format drift for its own sake: the identity is the asset.

## Recommended for the operator (outside the pipeline)

Publish on a fixed schedule; cut a 45-60 s Short from each episode's hook; put episodes in playlists by category; pin a question as the first comment; turn off auto-dubbing if machine-translated audio is not wanted; share each episode where its topic fits (engineering, history-of-tech and Indian-history communities) without spamming.
