# Compliance, licensing and integrity

An automated channel can produce a policy problem 365 times faster than a human one. These rules are not optional; they protect the account.

## Copyright

**Music:** this channel uses none (see `ambience-sound.md`); the synthesized ambience effects are generated locally and carry no licence. If music is ever added: only tracks you have the right to use — YouTube Audio Library, a paid subscription library with a licence covering the channel, Creative Commons tracks whose attribution requirements you actually follow, or generated music you have rights to. Keep a `brand/music_licences.md` log of every track, its source and its licence terms. Never use commercial music "quietly in the background" — audio fingerprinting doesn't care how quiet it is.

**Images and footage:** for the 3–8 real images per video, prefer public-domain and openly licensed sources (Wikimedia Commons, NASA, national archives, museum open-access collections, Library of Congress, CC-licensed material). Record source and licence for each in `01_research/sources.md`, and put a small on-screen credit in the corner. If an image's licence is unclear, don't use it — draw it instead. Stickman illustration is your get-out-of-jail card for anything unlicensed.

**Never** reuse another creator's video clips, animation, script structure, or thumbnail composition. Study competitors for what's missing, never for what to copy.

## Synthetic content disclosure

This channel's narration and visuals are AI-generated. In the upload flow, answer the altered/synthetic content question honestly. Stylised animation generally doesn't require the disclosure the way realistic synthetic depictions of real people or events do — but the safe and correct behaviour here is: **disclose when the video contains anything that could be mistaken for a real recording of a real person, place or event, and never generate realistic synthetic depictions of real people at all.** Keeping everything in stickman style is what makes this simple.

Also state plainly in the channel About section that the videos are AI-produced. Audiences respond far better to a channel that says so than to one they catch out.

## The "inauthentic content" problem — read this seriously

YouTube's monetisation policies require content to be original and authentic; mass-produced or repetitive material assembled with minimal transformation is a known route to demonetisation. A daily AI channel sits closer to that line than a weekly human one, so build distance from it deliberately:

- Original scripts with a specific point of view, real research and cited sources — never rewrites of a Wikipedia article read aloud.
- A distinctive, owned visual identity (which is exactly what the character and style lock give you).
- Real value per video: one mechanism genuinely explained.
- Variation between videos in structure and pacing, not a template stamped 365 times.
- Human oversight in the loop — the operator should watch each video before or shortly after publish and be able to veto.

If a video would be indistinguishable from a thousand other auto-generated uploads, it isn't finished.

## Factual integrity

- No invented quotes, dates, statistics or events. Ever. (See `research-and-facts.md`.)
- No fabricated sources in the description.
- Label speculation as speculation, in the narration, not just in the description.
- Correct errors publicly and quickly.

## Sensitive topics

Weapons and warfare topics (the atomic bomb, chemical weapons, missile guidance) are legitimate history and belong on the channel — but stay at the level of encyclopaedic explanation of *significance and consequence*. Never provide construction detail, synthesis routes, or anything that functions as instructions. If a script starts drifting toward "how you would actually build it", rewrite the section around the human and historical story instead.

Living people: factual and neutral. No mockery, no implied statements, no speculation about private matters.

Avoid: medical claims, financial advice framed as guidance, and current political controversy. This is a history-of-technology channel; it doesn't need them.

## Account safety

- Never buy views, subscribers, or engagement.
- Never use bots, pods, or automated comment posting on other channels.
- Never change account security settings.
- Nothing is posted by this pipeline; if a policy notice, strike or monetisation change is mentioned in operator notes, treat it as a reason to stop and ask.
